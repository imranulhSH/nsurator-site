Contact us

For problems, suggestions or partnerships, email support@nsurator.com, in English or Chinese. If the app opens, reporting from inside it is easier: the version and diagnostics are attached for you, so you only need to say what happened.

Report a problem on the Mac

  1. Choose Help → Report a Problem… in the menu bar. For a playback problem, you can also press ⌥⌘I while the player window is in front to open playback diagnostics, then click Report This Problem…; a card that says playback failed has a Report This Problem button too.
  2. Pick a category and write a line about what happened. Add screenshots or screen recordings if you have them (up to 3).
  3. Click Send. The report goes straight to Nsurator's own report service, not through email. If you are offline, it waits and is sent once you are back online. You get a number like 4F2A-9C1E.

Help → Sent Reports shows where each report stands (Received → In progress → Resolved) and any reply from the developer, and lets you add more with Add Details… When the developer replies, you'll see it under the bell in the menu bar and in Window → Notifications.

Report a problem on iPhone or iPad

  1. Open Manage → Report a Problem (it is also under Help).
  2. Write a line about what happened and tap Send.
  3. The report is sent by Mail to support@nsurator.com, with the diagnostics attached. If Mail isn't set up on the device, just email us directly.

What a report contains

  • The category you chose, what you wrote, and any screenshots or recordings you added.
  • The app version, the device and system version, diagnostics, and the most recent part of the on-device playback log.
  • Diagnostics and logs may include the names and paths of items in Private Space. Credentials such as account names and passwords are filtered out. The app says so on the report window: “Includes diagnostic info that may contain Private Space names. Used only to fix the problem, never made public.”

Please don't attach video files to a report or an email. If we need a sample, we'll tell you how to send it. How reports are stored and for how long is in the Privacy Policy.

When you email us, please include

  • Your device model, system version and Nsuve version (on the Mac, under Settings → Account & About).
  • What happened and the steps that lead to it; a screen recording of about 15 seconds helps a lot.
  • For playback problems on the Mac, a screenshot of the diagnostics panel (⌥⌘I).

Subscriptions, refunds and invoices

Purchases, renewals, cancellations and invoices for Nsuve are all handled by Apple. We can't see your Apple Account and can't issue refunds ourselves, so requesting a refund from Apple is the fastest way. If you run into trouble with the request, write to us and we'll help explain the situation.

  • To manage a subscription: on iPhone and iPad, go to Settings → Apple Account → Subscriptions; on the Mac, use your account settings in the App Store. Manage Subscription in the app (on the Mac, under Settings → Account & About) opens the same page.
  • Buying Lifetime Pro does not stop a yearly subscription. You need to turn it off yourself; the app reminds you once, but it can't cancel for you and nothing is refunded automatically.
  • Family Sharing is not available at launch.

Frequently asked questions

Which devices does it run on, and what do I need?

Nsuve comes as a Mac app and an iPhone and iPad app:

  • Mac: macOS 14 or later, on a Mac with Apple silicon only.
  • iPhone and iPad: iOS 17 or iPadOS 17 or later. It's one app; on iPad it uses two columns.
  • iPhone Duo: uses the iPhone app, which looks like a phone when folded and opens into two columns.
  • Apple TV and Vision Pro: coming soon.

Get the Mac app from the Mac App Store and the iPhone and iPad app from the App Store; both are free downloads. What each device does is covered in the guide to using several devices.

Free includes everything except AI tool connections, with 15 plays a month:

  • A play counts once more than 10 seconds have actually played. Pausing and seeking don't count.
  • The count follows the calendar month and starts over on the 1st.
  • It's kept on each device, and each device counts its own.
  • Starting a 16th play shows the purchase page; whatever is already playing keeps playing.

Nsuve Pro removes the play limit and adds AI tool connections (AI agents are on the Mac for now). Current prices:

Yes, as long as they use the same Apple Account:

  • Pro yearly: all your devices.
  • Lifetime Pro · Mac: every Mac on that Apple Account.
  • Lifetime Pro · iPhone + iPad: every iPhone and iPad on that Apple Account.

How do I restore my purchase on a new device?

Sign in with the same Apple Account, open Nsuve and choose Restore Purchases:

  • Mac: under Settings → Account & About, and on the purchase page.
  • iPhone and iPad: on the Pro card at the top of Manage, and on the purchase page.

You don't pay again. If restoring doesn't work, check that the App Store is signed in to the account you bought with, then report a problem from the app.

Which media sources can each device connect to?

Once a source is connected, Nsuve only reads its folders, recognizes what's there and builds an index. Your files stay where they are and stream when you play them.

  • Mac: external drives and local folders; WebDAV, mounted shares (SMB / NFS), direct SMB, SFTP; Emby, Jellyfin, Plex; fnOS, Synology, QNAP, Nextcloud; DLNA / UPnP; Baidu Netdisk, 123 Cloud, OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive, MEGA, pCloud, MagentaCLOUD, HiDrive, Koofr, Icedrive, kDrive; and the synced folders of iCloud Drive and other cloud drives on the Mac.
  • iPhone and iPad: WebDAV, SMB, SFTP; Emby, Jellyfin, Plex; files on the device and locations in the Files app. NAS boxes such as Synology, QNAP and fnOS connect over SMB, WebDAV or SFTP. With Nsuve Link, you can also browse the library on a Mac on the same local network.

Direct cloud-drive connections are on the Mac only. FTP can no longer be added as a new source; FTP sources you added before keep working.

When you connect Emby, Jellyfin or Plex, Nsuve builds a page for that server. If the server is briefly out of reach, the page keeps what it last read, and playback picks up once the server is back. Step by step: the guide to connecting sources.

My NAS or media server won't connect. What can I check?
  • Make sure the device and the NAS are on the same network and the address is right.
  • Only the host is required. Leave the port empty to use the default port, and leave the user name empty to sign in as a guest.
  • The service you use (SMB, WebDAV or SFTP) has to be turned on on the NAS, and the account needs permission to read that shared folder.

Still stuck? Report a problem from the app; the diagnostics go along with it.

How does Nsuve recognize films? Do I need a TMDB key?

No. Nsuve comes with Nsurator Library DB, whose data comes from TMDB and Bangumi and is served to the app by Nsurator's own service, so recognition works right away. To recognize a file, the app sends search text, usually the title, year and season and episode numbers worked out from the file name. It never uploads the video.

If a folder has NFO files and artwork, you can use those first: each folder can be set to Local first, Online first or Local only.

To use your own TMDB key, add it on the Mac under Settings → Movies & TV → Recognize online, or on iPhone and iPad under Manage → Movie & Show Info. The key is stored in the Keychain on that device, and the app uses it to talk to TMDB directly. Once a working key is saved, the Ask setting has four choices: Nsurator Library DB First (the default), My TMDB First, Only Nsurator Library DB and Only My TMDB.

To recognize nothing online: on iPhone and iPad, turn off Recognize Online under Movie & Show Info; on the Mac, set the folder to Local only.

Which formats does it play?
  • Over thirty containers, including MKV, MP4, MOV, AVI, RMVB, WMV, FLV, TS / M2TS and VOB.
  • Video from H.264, HEVC (including 10-bit), AV1 and VP9 to older codecs such as RV30 / RV40, VC-1 and Theora.
  • Audio from AAC, AC-3 / E-AC-3, TrueHD and DTS-HD to FLAC, ALAC, APE and DSD.
  • HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision and HDR Vivid, tone-mapped to what your display can show.
  • Discs: unencrypted Blu-ray folders (BDMV), ISO images and DVD folders (VIDEO_TS), with titles, chapters and menus.
  • Subtitles: 13 formats including SRT, ASS / SSA (with effects), PGS, WebVTT and TTML, embedded or in separate files; separate subtitle files that aren't UTF-8 display correctly too.

HEVC, AV1 and 4K use hardware decoding; hardware AV1 decoding needs an M3 or A17 Pro chip or later. 8-bit H.264 at 1080p and below is decoded in software on purpose, across several threads.

Are my files or my library uploaded anywhere?

Your video files are not uploaded to Nsurator's servers. Your library, watch history, notes and face data stay on your device, and Nsuve needs no account.

What does leave the device is what you use: the search text sent to Nsurator Library DB for recognition; the NAS, media servers and cloud drives you connect yourself; iCloud sync, if you turn it on; and the problem reports you choose to send. The Privacy Policy spells out what each one sends and where.

How do AI agents work?

AI agents are a Pro feature on the Mac. On the AI Agents page in the workbench sidebar, turn on Allow AI & automation to connect, then use the command or configuration snippet the page gives you to connect a tool you already use. There are 10 presets: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Windsurf, VS Code, Gemini CLI, Qoder, WorkBuddy, Kimi and Grok. Nsuve never edits those tools' own settings.

  • The service listens only on this Mac's loopback address (127.0.0.1), never on your local network or the internet.
  • Seven permissions switch on and off separately: read the library, read playback state, control playback, edit info, people, archive files and move to Trash.
  • Recent Activity records what each call did, and you can undo it.
  • AI tools never see Private Space, and they can't add media sources that need a password or a sign-in.
  • Requests that change more than 10 items, touch files or write NFO files, or re-recognize a whole folder need you to click Allow in the app. That confirmation card hasn't shipped yet, so these requests don't run for now.
  • On Free, a tool can still connect, but the only answer it gets is that this is a Pro feature.

Step by step: the guide to connecting AI tools.

Can I take my library to a new Mac or a new drive?

Yes. On the Mac, go to Settings → Backup & Move:

  • A backup holds your library database, posters and stills, the info you edited, the face library, collections and favorites, and the folders you added. It does not include the videos themselves, your TMDB key, subscriptions and authorizations, or preview caches.
  • Private Space is left out by default. Turning it on asks for Touch ID once.
  • Restoring only merges and never overwrites. You see a preview of what will change first; if a few items can't be written, you can skip them and merge the rest.
  • Automatic backups run daily or weekly while the app is open, and the last 5 are kept.
  • New Mac: use Pack One to Take… to put everything in one file, then restore it on the new Mac. New drive: copy your films to it, point the media library location at it, and Nsuve matches the files up again.

Backups are .nsuvebackup files; older .nsuratorbackup files still open. On iPhone and iPad it's Manage → Backup and Restore, which makes .nsbackup files and keeps Private Space separately in .nsprivate; the Mac and the iPhone and iPad use different backup formats. More in the backup guide.

I bought the old Nsurator app. What now?

Nsuve is a new app, so purchases in the old Nsurator app ("Nsurator - Local video manager" on the Mac App Store) don't carry over automatically. If you bought Nsurator Pro there, email support@nsurator.com and we'll get in touch with you directly.

Obtaining source for LGPL components

FFmpeg, libbluray, libudfread and FriBidi (Mac), and AMSMB2 and libsmb2 (iPhone and iPad), are used under the LGPL 2.1 or later. The exact source archives used in our builds, our patches with dated change notices, the patched source trees that were compiled, the configure lines, build scripts and build records are published per platform and build as the GitHub Releases below; each release names the app versions it covers.

Earlier Mac builds linked the playback core statically into the app; their release is macos-ffmpeg-artifact-2026.09.24.1. All releases are listed at lgpl-sources.

On the Mac these libraries are linked dynamically: the app and its decoder helper load them from Contents/Frameworks/NsuratorPlayerCoreFFmpegRuntime.framework, and you can replace that framework with one built from the corresponding source, modified or not, without relinking the app. The bundled ffmpeg and ffprobe are separate programs and can be replaced directly with your own builds of the same source. Each release explains how to build, how to replace, and how to sign the app again afterwards.

Running ffmpeg -version on the ffmpeg bundled with the Mac app shows libavutil license: LGPL version 2.1 or later.

If you have trouble getting the source, email support@nsurator.com with the platform and app version.

What is not included

To avoid misunderstanding: Nsuve contains no analytics or tracking SDKs, no advertising components, and no self-update framework outside the App Store; updates are distributed only through the Mac App Store and the App Store. The playback components include no GPL encoders such as x264 / x265; H.264 encoding uses the system's VideoToolbox.
